A small workshop, a clear bargain

A fictional university club prepares limited seats and materials. Participation—and the deposit—is voluntary.

Form + bank transfer

  • Familiar attendance registration and payment tools.
  • The organizer holds deposits and reconciles refunds or rewards.
  • No wallet or testnet-gas onboarding.

CommitPot

  • Deposits follow fixed contract rules, not an organizer withdrawal decision.
  • Wallet-specific check-ins and beneficiary payments can be inspected.
  • Costs: wallet prompts, AVAX gas and a trusted human verifier.

The hypothesis is easier-to-inspect reconciliation—not proven time savings, safer competitors or a causal attendance effect.

Illustrative scenario; not a recruited event or user study2 / 8

Agree before depositing

  1. Create: fix token, stake, verifier and registration/check-in deadlines.
  2. Join: approve the exact stake, then deposit before registration closes. Gas is separate.
  3. Check in: the human verifier inspects the participant, attests presence, signs and submits. A signature alone is not a confirmed check-in.
  4. Settle: anyone can finalize after check-in closes. Eligible wallets claim; another caller cannot redirect their payment.

If at least one attends: only verified attendees share all recorded deposits. A no-show forfeits the stake.
If nobody attends: every joined wallet can reclaim its own stake.

Reward depends on attendance; it is not yield. Integer remainder units go to earliest check-in orders. No cancellation, policy edits, dispute process or verifier replacement.

No admin withdrawal ≠ no trust

What the contract fixes

  • No admin withdrawal or mutable pot settings in this implementation.
  • Eligibility, one claim per beneficiary and deterministic rounding.
  • Anyone may call a claim; tokens go only to its eligible beneficiary.
  • Recorded deposits—not donations—fund each pot’s entitlements.

What remains trusted

  • The verifier’s attendance judgment and availability.
  • Wallet, RPC, token behavior and the source/deployment match.
  • A copied QR identifies a wallet/pot; it proves neither presence nor wallet ownership.
  • After partial check-in, verifier loss can unfairly exclude others. No remedy exists here.

Test USDC can be subject to issuer pause/blacklist rules; Fuji token identity still needs verification. Development testing is not a formal security audit. No claim expiry or unclaimed-fund sweep.

Measure usability, not invented impact

A proposed consent-based, test-token exercise. Not yet conducted; no recruitment in this task.

Observe the work

  • Consent, understand the forfeiture rule, join, check in and claim.
  • Record wallet failures, retries, verifier attempts and reconciliation effort.
  • Separate unique people, fixture wallets, tasks and transaction attempts.

Keep the denominators

  • Report completions / starters, and claims / eligible wallets.
  • Keep failed, pending and abandoned attempts visible.
  • Record timing start/end definitions; report sample size with median and range.
  • No percentage or benefit claim before observations exist.

Worthless test stakes cannot establish a real no-show reduction. That causal question needs a separately approved comparative study.
